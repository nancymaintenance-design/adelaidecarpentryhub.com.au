Adelaide Heritage Timber Repairs: A Practical Scope for Doors, Windows and External Joinery

Heritage timber work rewards careful observation. The best first decision is often to retain and repair sound original material rather than replace it by default.

Record what is there before proposing a solution

Photograph each elevation and detail the parts that matter: profiles, mouldings, glazing bars, hardware, joints, paint build-up and areas of decay. Doors and windows may show localised damage at a sill, bottom rail or weather-exposed edge while much of the original joinery remains serviceable. A condition record helps distinguish a repairable component from one that has lost structural integrity.

Repair, splice or replace: make the decision visible

A good repair scope states which timber is retained, which decayed sections are cut back, how new timber is matched and where joints will be placed. This makes it easier to compare a local splice, a rebuilt sash or a complete replacement. Matching the visual profile is important, but so are the water-shedding detail, glazing, fixing method and paint system that will protect the work after installation.

Check heritage and planning requirements early

Some Adelaide properties are subject to heritage or planning controls. Requirements vary with the property and the proposed work, especially where external fabric or visible street-facing elements are affected. Before starting substantial alterations, confirm the current requirements with the relevant authority or a suitably qualified adviser. This article is a planning guide, not heritage or legal advice.

Give the repair a maintenance plan

Timber lasts longer when paint failure, failed sealant, blocked drainage paths and exposed end grain are addressed early. At handover, ask what has been repaired, which finish system has been used, when it should be reviewed and which warning signs—such as cracking, sticking, water entry or soft timber—should prompt another inspection.
