Buyer Guide

Carpenter, joiner or cabinet maker: who does your project need?

Choose by the work and its interfaces: a carpenter handles site timber work, a joiner creates fitted timber details, and a cabinet maker focuses on cabinets and storage. The scope can overlap, so confirm each provider’s actual responsibilities.

Editorial guidance by MEL ONE

Start with the object and the stage of work

Site carpentry may involve framing, doors, trims or renovation timber work. Architectural joinery involves fitted features and detailed timber elements; cabinet making focuses on kitchens, wardrobes and other storage. These descriptions are a starting point, not proof of a provider’s capability. List the items you need and ask who will measure, manufacture, install and finish each one.

Coordination is a responsibility to agree

One contact can make communication simpler, but a combined quote does not establish that every trade is delivered by the same person. Ask which work is included, which specialists are separately engaged and who resolves the junctions. A clear allocation of responsibilities matters whether you appoint one provider or several.

Check evidence against the proposed work

Ask for relevant work examples and a written description of the proposed scope. Confirm who contracts the work, who supervises it where required and how to check the relevant licence details. A workshop, a broad service list or a company registration alone does not establish authority for every type of building work.

Where coordination usually breaks down

The high-risk moments are rarely dramatic: a wall is lined before a niche is measured, a benchtop is ordered before services are set, or a door swing conflicts with a cabinet installed later. These gaps occur when each trade works from a different assumption. A coordinated carpentry and joinery process makes the interfaces visible before they become site fixes.

One measure does not mean one guess

Integrated delivery is not about taking a single measurement and hoping it survives the build. It means revisiting critical dimensions at the right stage, documenting changes and deciding when workshop production should begin. Site conditions, linings, floor finishes and services can all affect the final fit, so the sequence matters as much as the initial design.

How to compare scopes, not just totals

When comparing quotes, look for who owns set-out, site measure, shop drawings, material selection, protection, installation, adjustments and handover. A low initial figure may exclude the coordination that makes a complex room fit together. A useful quote names assumptions and interfaces so the owner can see what must be resolved before work starts.

A practical project workflow

For a renovation or new build, the process normally moves from brief and drawings to site verification, detail decisions, production timing, staged installation and final adjustment. Not every project needs a large formal programme, but every project benefits from agreeing who makes decisions, when dimensions are confirmed and how late changes will be managed.

Questions to ask before appointing a team

Ask what work is completed in-house, who will be your site contact, how measurements are verified, when custom pieces are released for manufacture and how defects or changes are handled. Clear answers demonstrate process knowledge. They are more informative than broad claims about quality because they show how a team protects quality under real project conditions.

Choosing a carpentry and joinery scope

Does one provider need to do all the timber work?

No. The useful question is whether site dimensions, drawings, sequencing and responsibility are agreed. Separate providers can work together when their interfaces are documented.

Is a kitchen cabinet quote the same as a renovation quote?

No. A cabinetry quote can cover cabinet construction and installation while demolition, services, surfaces and making-good are separately agreed. Compare the included items before comparing totals.

When should final joinery measurements be taken?

At the stage when the relevant walls, floors, openings and service positions can be verified. Agree the measuring and drawing approval points before releasing custom pieces for manufacture.

How do I check a proposed contractor’s licence?

Use the Consumer and Business Services register to check the licence holder and relevant details, then confirm that the proposed work and contracting arrangements match. Company identity alone does not answer that question.
