Wardrobe Measurements and Usable Clearances

Overall width, height and depth are only the first layer. Note skirtings, cornices, power points, windows, light switches, return walls and door swings, plus any variation in floor or wall level. In older Adelaide homes, walls and ceilings are not always square; an on-site measure before manufacture is the point at which the cabinet dimensions, filler panels and scribe details can be finalised.

The internal depth, drawer projection, hanging clearance and shelf spacing must work with the garments and room, not just fit a nominal cabinet size. A wardrobe can lose useful capacity when a door, bed or window restricts access. Discussing these constraints early allows the layout to favour the items used daily and reserve higher or deeper areas for occasional storage.

Filler panels, scribes and small shadow lines are often the practical way for cabinetry to meet existing walls that are not straight. They should be treated as considered details, not defects. Reviewing samples, handle positions and the intended reveal between doors gives everyone a shared expectation before a custom wardrobe is manufactured and installed.

Internal Layout for Daily and Future Storage

List what the wardrobe must hold before selecting finishes. Long-hang clothing, folded garments, shoes, linen, bags, drawers and seasonal storage each need different clearances. Mix dedicated hanging zones with adjustable shelves and removable rails where future needs may change, such as a child growing or a room becoming a study. Size drawers for the items they will hold, and avoid deep, unlit shelves that become dead space rather than adding every available accessory.

A hypothetical bedroom brief might put long hanging space on one side and adjustable shelves on the other. Check the actual garments, drawer opening and room circulation before agreeing that arrangement.

Wardrobe Doors, Materials, Lighting and Ventilation

Hinged doors need clear swing space; sliding doors preserve circulation but limit access to part of the interior at once. Mirrored, painted and timber-look finishes each change light, maintenance and the visual weight of the room. For wardrobes near bathrooms, laundries or external walls, discuss moisture exposure and ventilation so the substrate, edging and installation detail suit the location.

Integrated lighting, power points and ventilation can make a wardrobe easier to use, but they need to be planned before the unit is built. Identify what requires electrical coordination, where cables or drivers can be accessed and whether exterior walls or adjoining wet areas create condensation concerns. These interfaces are resolved with the appropriate trades rather than retrofitted blindly.

Access, Site Measure and the Manufacturing Brief

Confirm access from the street to the room, stair turns, parking, flooring protection and the timing of painting, electrical work and new carpet. A clear installation sequence reduces the risk that joinery is measured before adjoining finishes are ready. To request a quote, tell MEL ONE what the wardrobe needs to store and the finishes you prefer. Existing room photos, a rough sketch and approximate measurements are optional; email them to handymanfelix.au2026@outlook.com if available. MEL ONE confirms the room and access measurements on site.

Use the site measurements to check walls, floors and access against the proposed internal layout. Review door movement, drawer projection, hardware clearances, filler details and any lighting interfaces together. Approve the drawings and specification before the final quoted scope proceeds to manufacture and installation.

Wardrobe Quote Inclusions and Approved Changes

The quote should describe the wardrobe location, layout and quantities: hanging zones, shelves, drawers and any selected accessories. Identify the cabinet substrate, edging, visible finishes, door material, handles and hardware. Confirm whether removal of an existing unit, transport, floor and room protection, waste removal, installation, wall or trim making-good, and lighting interfaces are included. Record exclusions and responsibilities clearly so the total scope is easy to compare.

An early budget estimate uses the information and selections available at that stage. A confirmed quote follows the site measure and agreed layout, materials, hardware and installation conditions. Where something still needs checking, record the assumption and how it will be resolved. Ask MEL ONE to explain the pricing basis and included costs rather than treating a photo-based estimate as an approved manufacturing specification.

Adding drawers, changing hinged doors to sliding doors or selecting a different door material can change hardware, internal access, fabrication and installation work. MEL ONE records the revised layout or specification and explains the effect on price and scheduling in a written variation. Approve that change before the affected work proceeds; keep the approved version alongside the original quote.

Wardrobe Handover Checks and Records

At handover, compare the installed layout and visible finish with the approved scope and final specification. Operate the doors and drawers; check handles, shelving, alignment, visible edges, agreed lighting access and how the unit meets the floor, walls and ceiling. Record any adjustment or outstanding work while the installation team is available, and keep the specification and surface-care information with the completion record.

Custom wardrobe planning questions

What measurements are needed for a wardrobe quote, and are photos enough?

For planning, note the available width, height and depth, ceiling slopes, skirtings, door swings and other obstructions if you already have that information. Photos and approximate measurements are optional for the first enquiry and can help MEL ONE discuss an initial budget. Room photos alone do not establish the final manufacturing dimensions or price. The confirmed quote follows the site measure, agreed design and specification, and installation arrangements.

What happens if I add drawers or change the doors?

We revise the layout, materials and hardware requirements, explain the effect on price and scheduling, and obtain approval for a written variation before the affected work proceeds.

Does the wardrobe quote include removal and making-good?

We identify removal, transport, protection, waste, installation and making-good as included, excluded or separately arranged in the written quote. Tell us what needs removing and which adjoining finishes must be retained so the site measure can address those details.

What should I keep after installation?

Keep the approved design and specification, quote and variations, care information, and handover record showing completed work and any agreed follow-up.
