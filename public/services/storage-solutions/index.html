Wardrobe Internal Layout: Fit the Belongings and the Room

A built-in wardrobe should be planned around long and short hanging, folded items, shoes, drawers and occasional storage. An awkward alcove or sloping ceiling can reduce usable depth even when the front looks generous. MEL ONE measures the space and develops the internal arrangement around the household's storage priorities.

Compare hinged and sliding doors against the bed, walkway and drawer positions. Hinged doors need swing space and offer broad access; sliding doors preserve circulation while covering part of the interior at a time. Adjustable shelves and removable fittings can accommodate changing needs, but selected accessories should earn their place through useful storage rather than appearance alone.

Built-in Bookshelves and Home-office Joinery

A bookcase carrying books needs a different shelf and fixing brief from light display storage. Material, thickness, shelf span, support and the wall construction all influence the arrangement, so intended loads need to be discussed before manufacture. The quote should identify the shelf layout and fixing approach rather than promise one capacity for every unit.

For a study, plan desk depth, chair movement, screens, drawers and cable access together. A media wall or display unit also needs equipment dimensions, ventilation and a route to reach connections later. Electrical, data, lighting and cooling equipment need separately specified work by the relevant trades.

Under-stair Storage: Usable Depth and Retained Access

The slope behind the front elevation determines whether cupboards, drawers or open shelves provide useful access. Check how far a drawer can project and whether a door conflicts with the hall or adjacent room. A deep void may need a different internal arrangement from a shallow display recess.

Identify service panels and other access that must remain reachable, and check walls and fixing conditions before selecting the layout. Storage installation does not authorise cutting stair supports or altering a barrier. Any structural change or specialist requirement needs its own resolved design and scope.

Measured Drawings, Materials and Installation

Rough room measurements are useful for planning, but the final measure records wall variation, floors, ceiling height, skirtings and installation access. Fillers and scribes allow a measured unit to meet an irregular existing room; their intended appearance should be clear on the approved detail. Large units may also need delivery and stair-turn checks before manufacture.

Agree cabinet and door materials, edging, handles, hinges, runners and visible finishes with the layout. Samples help when new storage meets existing joinery or flooring. Before handover, review doors, drawers, shelving, visible edges and any retained service access against the agreed specification.

Wardrobe and Fitted-storage Quote Inclusions

Compare the same hanging zones, shelves, drawers, door type, materials and hardware across quotes. Identify removal of an existing unit, transport, room protection, installation, waste and adjoining making-good. Lighting, power changes and equipment ventilation remain distinct inclusions or separately arranged work.

Extra drawers, changed door types and different runners can change the price without changing the unit's overall size. Compare the internal layout and hardware schedule as well as the elevation. Approve changes before manufacture, then review door travel, drawer access and retained service access at handover.

Wardrobe Doors, Measurements and Joinery Quote Questions

Should I choose sliding or hinged wardrobe doors?

Hinged doors need swing space and give broad access; sliding doors preserve room circulation but cover part of the interior at a time. Compare both against the bed, walkway and drawer positions.

Are my rough wardrobe measurements enough for manufacture?

Rough dimensions are useful for discussing a layout, but final manufacture needs a site measure. Record wall and floor variation, ceiling slopes, skirtings, openings and delivery access, then approve the internal layout, door clearances and filler details. Photos and approximate dimensions are optional for the first enquiry and do not establish final cabinet sizes or the confirmed price.

What belongs in a bookcase or home-office joinery quote?

Record the intended use, shelf depths, likely loads, drawers, doors, desk and cable access, materials and hardware. Confirm site measurement, supports, installation, removal, finishing and any electrical work separately. For under-stair storage, identify access panels and structural elements that must remain untouched.
