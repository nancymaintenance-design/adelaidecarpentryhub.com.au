Storage Solutions

MEL ONE plans built-in wardrobes and storage around what needs a place and how the room is used. Review layout, door movement, shelves, finishes and installation access before approving the joinery.

Walk-in wardrobes

Hanging, drawer, shoe and luggage zones with adjustable shelving that grows with you.

Study units

Desk + bookcase + storage combos with hidden cable management, ergonomic height and monitor-arm provision.

Media walls

TV wall mounts, speaker gear, media storage and decor in one wall, with cooling and concealed cabling.

Built-in bookshelves and home-office joinery

Plan a bookcase around what it holds: books, folders, equipment and display items have different depths, weights and access needs. For a home-office unit, include the desk, chair movement, screen position and cable route rather than filling every wall with shelving. We measure the space and resolve supports, shelf spans, wall fixing and material choices for the intended use before manufacture. Lighting, power changes and equipment ventilation need their own agreed arrangements.

Under-stair storage without losing useful access

The slope, available depth, door swing and nearby circulation determine whether drawers, cupboards or open shelving are practical. Identify any existing services and access panels that must remain reachable. Measure the usable interior, not only the front elevation, and confirm the walls and fixing conditions. Storage work must not assume permission to alter stair structure, barriers or required access.

Display & wine cabinets

Display joinery can be planned around selected lighting or wine-storage equipment. Electrical work, cooling equipment, ventilation and service access require separate specification and responsible trades.

Map what needs to be stored

Storage works when it reflects actual belongings and routines. We ask about hanging lengths, folded items, files, toys, shoes, media equipment, cleaning tools and the objects clients want to display. Mapping these categories before design helps avoid beautiful cabinetry that has too little depth, too few drawers or inaccessible high shelves.

Plan movement, reach and access

Doors, drawers and pull-outs need room to operate without blocking a bed, walkway, desk or adjacent cabinet. We consider everyday reach, clear floor area, opening direction and where power or ventilation is needed. The result is storage that supports the room rather than forcing people to work around it every time they use it.

Internal fittings that earn their space

Adjustable shelves, hanging rails, drawers, pull-out baskets, integrated lighting and cable management each have a useful role when selected for a specific need. We discuss which fittings simplify daily use and which add cost without adding value. This keeps the design focused on durable, serviceable storage rather than features chosen only for a showroom effect.

Walls, services and mounting conditions

Tall units, media walls and floating elements need to suit the wall construction, service routes and load they will carry. We check site conditions before finalising fixing and access details. Electrical, data, ventilation and other specialist services are coordinated with the relevant trades so the finished unit remains practical to use and maintain.

Finishes that suit the room

A wardrobe, study or media unit should relate to light, flooring, adjoining joinery and the amount of wear it will receive. We can discuss timber, veneer, painted, laminate and glass elements in terms of appearance, cleaning and repairability. Samples help guide colour and texture decisions, particularly where new joinery meets an existing interior.

Preparing for a custom-storage consultation

Photos, a rough room plan, ceiling height, wall dimensions and a list of what needs to be stored are an excellent starting point. Include appliances, screens or equipment that must fit, and note access restrictions in apartments or older homes. We measure the room and clearances on site, develop the storage layout around your needs and confirm the materials, installation scope and written quote.

Plan usable built-in storage — questions

Should I choose sliding or hinged wardrobe doors?

Hinged doors need swing space and give broad access; sliding doors preserve room circulation but cover part of the interior at a time. Compare both against the bed, walkway and drawer positions.

Are my rough wardrobe measurements enough for manufacture?

They are useful for the first discussion, but manufacture follows a site measure and approved layout. Wall variation, skirtings, cornices and floor levels affect fillers and the final dimensions.

What belongs in a bookcase or home-office joinery quote?

Record the intended use, shelf depths, likely loads, drawers, doors, desk and cable access, materials and hardware. Confirm site measurement, supports, installation, removal, finishing and any electrical work separately. For under-stair storage, identify access panels and structural elements that must remain untouched.
