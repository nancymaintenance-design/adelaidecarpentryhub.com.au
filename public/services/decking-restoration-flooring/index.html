Existing Decks: Repair Before Sanding or Oiling

Peeling coating and a few split boards can call for a different scope from a deck with soft supports or widespread movement. MEL ONE checks the accessible boards, fixings, edges and substructure before recommending a surface treatment. A fresh finish cannot restore a weakened member or prove that the deck is suitable to keep using.

Compare local board repair, component replacement and a broader replacement brief against the condition found. Deck steps, barriers and supports need their safety and structural roles considered where affected. A completely new deck or changed outdoor layout belongs in a separate design and installation brief rather than being assumed in restoration pricing.

Indoor Timber Floor Repairs and Floorboard Replacement

A leaking appliance, split board or local worn patch needs the floor product and damage identified before a repair method is chosen. Solid, engineered and floating arrangements can have different fixing and replacement limits. Check the board profile, thickness, access and the source of wetting before closing a water-damaged area.

Local floorboard replacement may be suitable where the surrounding floor and supports can be retained. Grain, age and colour can leave the new area visible, so wider sanding or finishing is a separate choice. Persistent bounce, a dipping floor or support deterioration needs its own subfloor assessment instead of being covered by a new board or coating.

Sanding, Coatings and the Achievable Finish

The remaining wear layer, previous treatments and current surface condition determine whether sanding is appropriate. The selected preparation and coating must be compatible with the identified product. A stain or oil can change appearance, but it cannot guarantee an identical colour across old boards, new repairs and areas with different exposure.

Discuss the desired result using the actual room or deck, with a sample or test area where useful. For outdoor boards, consider weather, drainage, exposure and cleaning as well as colour. Agree finish-care and slip-treatment expectations for the selected system without treating a coating as a structural repair.

Floor and Deck Work: Access, Edges and Trade Sequence

Indoor work may involve furniture, thresholds, skirtings and junctions to carpet or tile, while external work needs weather and underside access considered. Identify which boards or edge details need repair and who handles removal, protection and reinstatement. The quote should separate indoor flooring from outdoor decking even when both are in one enquiry.

Plan installation or surface treatment around other trades and the product's preparation and drying requirements. Completed areas need appropriate protection before furniture or traffic returns. Do not enter an unsafe deck underside to take photographs; explain any access limits so the assessment can identify what remains unseen.

A Restoration Quote and Care Plan

Ask for board repair or replacement, fixings, preparation, sanding and coating to be identified separately. Confirm the area treated, finish, waste, protection, furniture handling and any adjoining trim work. A cheaper surface-only quote is difficult to compare with one that also resolves damaged boards or connections unless those differences are clear.

Product limits, furniture handling and the area refinished can change the allowance even for the same damaged boards. Compare repair and surface-treatment items separately. At handover, keep care and drying guidance for the selected finish and record outstanding support or moisture work.

Deck Refinishing and Floorboard Replacement Questions

Can sanding fix a soft or moving deck?

Sanding changes the surface; it does not restore decayed supports or repair a failed connection. Inspect the accessible boards, fixings, edges and substructure before deciding whether refinishing is appropriate. Local repairs may need to happen first, while widespread movement or deterioration can require a broader assessment and a separately defined replacement scope.

Can damaged floorboards be replaced without refinishing the whole room?

Sometimes a local replacement is suitable, but the floor type, board thickness, fixing, accessible damage and finish decide the scope. A replacement can remain visibly different. Engineered products also depend on the remaining wear layer and manufacturer guidance; neither sanding nor a local patch is a universal solution.
