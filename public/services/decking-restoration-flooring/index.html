Decking Restoration & Timber Flooring

MEL ONE scopes existing deck restoration and indoor timber-floor work as different tasks. Establish timber condition and the cause of wear before choosing repairs, sanding, recoating or replacement.

Timber floor laying

Solid and engineered timber with damp-proof underlay, expansion gaps and accurate levelling.

Deck sanding & oiling

Deck preparation and coating depend on timber condition, existing finish and product compatibility. Resolve decay or movement first, then agree appropriate surface treatment and a care plan.

Floor refinishing

Floor refinishing depends on product identification, remaining wear layer, previous treatments and surface condition. Agree the achievable finish and protection requirements before sanding.

Indoor timber floor repairs and floorboard replacement

An indoor floor repair starts with the affected boards, not the choice of oil. Describe loose, split, worn or water-affected floorboards and whether movement extends beyond one area. We inspect accessible condition and identify the floor type, board profile, thickness and finish before agreeing local repair or replacement. Matching grain and colour may leave a visible difference; sanding or finishing a wider area is a separate decision. Persistent bounce, sagging or moisture needs its own assessment rather than a cosmetic cover-up.

Keep the floor-repair quote separate from deck work

For an indoor floor, record the rooms, repair areas, furniture handling, thresholds, skirting interfaces and any agreed sanding or coating. For an outdoor deck, record exposed boards, accessible supports, weather and access. These are different work scopes even when both involve timber. Ask which removal, waste, replacement boards and finishing items are included, and how hidden damage will be documented and approved before the scope changes.

Assess the timber beneath the finish

Before recommending sanding, coating or replacement, we look at the condition that can be seen: board movement, staining, worn coating, gaps, previous repairs, moisture clues and access to edges. A timber surface may need cosmetic work, local repair or a broader subfloor investigation. Understanding the difference helps owners choose an appropriate scope rather than applying a finish over an unresolved problem.

Choose an outcome, not just a colour

The desired result might be a refreshed natural look, a more even tone, reduced slipperiness, a darker stain or a finish that is easier to maintain. We discuss how timber species, existing coatings, sunlight and prior repairs affect those choices. Samples and test areas can be useful when a client wants to see how a proposed product reads in the actual room or outdoor setting.

Prepare the space and manage interfaces

Floor and deck work is affected by furniture, access, weather, dust control, adjacent finishes and curing time. We plan practical preparation with the client or builder so the work area can be accessed safely and completed surfaces are protected. External work is scheduled with realistic attention to weather and drying conditions rather than promises that ignore the site environment.

Repair boards, edges and transitions

A quality refresh often includes more than the open field of boards. Edges, thresholds, loose fixings, local damage, stair nosings and junctions to tile or carpet may need attention to make the completed surface feel resolved. We discuss these details upfront because they affect both appearance and the practical sequence of the work.

Care after sanding, oiling or coating

Newly treated timber benefits from the right early care and a realistic maintenance routine. We explain what to avoid while finishes settle, how to clean without unnecessary abrasion and what signs suggest it is time to review a high-wear or high-exposure area. The useful interval depends on the timber, finish, traffic, sun and moisture—not a generic calendar promise.

Information for a flooring or deck review

Describe the flooring or deck condition and your preferred finish. Existing daylight photos, approximate area, material or coating history are optional; do not enter an unsafe underside to photograph it. We inspect the timber, fixings, moisture exposure and drainage on site and confirm maintenance, repair or replacement work and the quote.

Describe the deck or indoor floor — questions

Can sanding fix a soft or moving deck?

Sanding changes the surface; it does not restore a weakened support or resolve movement. Assess boards, accessible substructure and moisture conditions before deciding whether cosmetic restoration is suitable.

Can damaged floorboards be replaced without refinishing the whole room?

Sometimes a local replacement is suitable, but the floor type, board thickness, fixing, accessible damage and finish decide the scope. A replacement can remain visibly different. Engineered products also depend on the remaining wear layer and manufacturer guidance; neither sanding nor a local patch is a universal solution.
