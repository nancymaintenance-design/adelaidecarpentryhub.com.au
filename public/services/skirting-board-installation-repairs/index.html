Skirting Board Installation & Repairs

MEL ONE installs, replaces and repairs Adelaide skirting boards. Start with the affected lengths and existing profile, then coordinate wall condition, finished floor height and painting responsibilities.

New skirting, replacement and local repair

Skirting work can range from replacing a damaged length to fitting a consistent profile through a renovated room or full home. The right scope depends on what is already in place, whether the wall line is changing and how the skirting meets door jambs, stairs, cabinetry and flooring. We discuss whether matching, selective repair or a new continuous treatment best suits the visible condition and the wider finishing work.

Profiles, material and the existing room

A skirting profile changes the character of a room and the way trim joins read at corners and openings. Existing height, shape, material, paint build-up and wall condition all affect a matching decision. Where a profile is being selected for a new space, it should be considered alongside architraves, doors, cabinetry and floor finishes rather than in isolation. Samples, photos and a clear reference point help avoid a result that looks unrelated to adjoining rooms.

Floor levels and practical transitions

Skirtings sit at the meeting point of wall and floor, so final floor thickness, carpet, timber, tile and threshold details matter. A room may have uneven floors, newly installed flooring or an existing gap from an earlier finish. We review those transitions before fixing the trim, particularly where a new floor will follow after carpentry. This lets the scope allow for neat junctions without treating a changing floor level as an afterthought.

Corners, returns and irregular walls

Older walls, external corners, bay windows, stair strings and built-ins need careful measuring. MEL ONE checks the room and plans cutting, returns and joins for a consistent skirting finish. We explain any wall or floor adjustments in the agreed scope and quote.

Repairing damaged or missing sections

Water, moving furniture, pets, previous alterations and removed cabinetry can leave skirting with chips, breaks, stains or missing lengths. A local repair may be appropriate where the surrounding profile and material are sound. When damage is widespread, a longer replacement section may create a more consistent result. We assess what can be seen and make clear where concealed wall, floor or moisture conditions could affect the final extent of work.

Coordination with paint, doors and cabinetry

The most successful interior finish comes from sequencing. Painting, flooring, door hanging, joinery installation and skirting work can affect one another, especially where caulking, filling, coating and touch-up responsibilities differ. Let us know what stage the room is at and which trades are still to follow. We can then discuss when trim should be measured and installed, and how it will connect to new door jambs, wardrobes or kitchen cabinetry.

Details that help us prepare the site measure

Tell MEL ONE whether you need skirting repair, profile matching or new installation, and which rooms are involved. Photos, approximate dimensions and a sketch are optional for first contact; existing floor-finish and planned-door details help us prepare. We measure the junctions on site, check wet areas, curves and built-ins and confirm the profile, work scope and written quote.

Plan the skirting lengths and profile — questions

Can just one damaged skirting length be replaced?

Yes, where the adjacent material and fixing surface are suitable. Matching depends on profile availability, existing paint build-up and finish; a new length may need a wider painting allowance.

Should skirting go in before or after new flooring?

The flooring system, final thickness and agreed junction determine the order. Confirm those details with the flooring trade before trim is measured and fixed.

Is skirting painting included in the carpentry price?

Ask the quote to identify removal, wall repairs, fixing, filling, caulking and coating separately. A paint-ready allowance should describe the actual preparation rather than imply a completed painted finish.
