Door & Window Repairs

MEL ONE repairs existing timber doors and windows in Adelaide, starting with what has changed. We check the leaf or sash, hardware, exposed timber and surrounding opening before agreeing adjustment, local repair or replacement scope.

What door and window repair can cover

A repair can involve a timber door leaf, frame, jamb, sash, sill, architrave or local trim. The useful starting point is the part that is actually affected: a door may stick because of movement, a damaged edge, hardware adjustment or a changed floor level, while a window problem may relate to a sash, frame, weather-exposed timber or surrounding finish. We discuss the observed condition before assuming a single cause.

Common signs worth recording

Owners may notice rubbing doors, misaligned latches, loose trim, peeling paint, soft timber, difficult windows or draughts. Tell MEL ONE what has changed, including whether it follows rain or seasonal movement. Safely taken whole-opening and detail photos are optional. We check the timber, hardware and adjoining surfaces on site and confirm local repair, adjustment or any wider condition assessment required.

Condition, water and surrounding materials

Timber deterioration can be local, but it is important to understand the conditions around it. Water paths, failed coatings, end grain, flashing, glazing, seals and junctions to masonry or cladding can all influence a repair decision. Replacing a visibly damaged piece without considering why it deteriorated may leave the same exposure unresolved. We keep the carpentry scope clear and identify adjoining glazing, coating or building-envelope work that may need coordination.

Timber window and sash repairs

For a timber window repair, identify the sash, sill, frame and opening mechanism. A soft timber window sill, rotten lower frame, painted-shut sash and a window dropping shut are different faults. MEL ONE checks accessible timber and operation before comparing local repair, component replacement and a wider repair scope. Keeping original windows can be an option when enough suitable material remains; glazing, major frame distortion and extensive decay need separate assessment.

Sash cords, weights and spiral balances

Traditional sash windows use cords, pulleys and counterweights; other systems use spring or spiral balances. MEL ONE first identifies the original mechanism. For a cord-and-weight repair, agreed work can include necessary bead or access-cover removal, cord replacement and reconnection, pulley checks and adjustment of the sash, beads and sliding clearance. Balance replacements are matched to sash weight, mechanism dimensions and installation arrangement, then fitted, adjusted and tested according to the product requirements. Similar appearance alone is not a sufficient parts match.

A sash that drops shut or is painted closed

A sash that falls when released needs its support mechanism assessed; forcing or repeatedly testing a heavy unstable sash can be unsafe. A painted-shut window may also involve the beads, clearances or timber condition rather than one broken cord. We check agreed repairs for smooth travel, operation on both sides, stable stopping and correct latch and closing positions. Discontinued parts, broken glazing, major distortion, widespread rot or special heritage window work are separately assessed, not automatically included in a cord replacement.

Cavity sliding doors: access before adjustment

A timber cavity slider can rub, drag or fail to latch because of its rollers, track, guides, leaf or surrounding opening. The useful first check is what can be reached without disturbing the wall or finishes. Note whether the fault occurs throughout travel or at one point, and whether flooring has changed. We inspect the accessible door and confirm the repair scope, hardware availability and any opening-up or making-good needed. Automatic doors, garage doors and mainly aluminium or glass systems need their own specialist scope.

Repair, local replacement or a new element

The right approach depends on how much sound material remains, the visible profile, daily use and the purpose of the opening. Some problems can be resolved with careful adjustment or a local timber repair; others need a replacement component or a wider discussion about the frame and adjoining finish. For older Adelaide homes, maintaining proportions and interfaces can matter as much as restoring operation. We explain the practical options rather than presenting cosmetic replacement as the only answer.

Planning access and hardware

Doors and windows are used every day, so access and timing affect the work. Tell us which rooms are involved, whether the opening must remain secure or weather-protected, the type of hardware present and whether the work is part of painting, glazing or a renovation. Photos of hinges, latches, locks and problem areas help establish the likely carpentry tasks. Specialist hardware, glazing or security requirements should be identified early so the correct trade interfaces can be planned.

Working with heritage and existing joinery

MEL ONE inspects older timber openings, records the profiles and joints, and identifies sound material to retain and damaged sections to repair or replicate. We check the repair requirements and discuss the project documents, access and approved scope with you before work begins. Contact our Adelaide team to arrange an assessment and quote.

What should I record about a sticking cavity sliding door?

Note where it catches, whether the door has dropped and whether flooring or finishes changed. A site assessment checks the accessible leaf, guides, rollers and track before a repair is agreed. Concealed access and making-good must be included explicitly; do not force a jammed or unstable door.

What is the difference between sash cords and spiral balances?

Sash cords connect a traditional window sash to counterweights through pulleys. Spring or spiral balances support a sash using a different mechanism. MEL ONE identifies the existing system and matches parts to the sash weight, dimensions and installation requirements; a similar-looking balance is not necessarily compatible.
