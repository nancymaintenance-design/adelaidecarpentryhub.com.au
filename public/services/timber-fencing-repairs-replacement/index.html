Timber Fencing Repairs & Replacement

MEL ONE assesses timber fence boards, rails and posts before recommending local repair or a replacement section. Discuss both sides of the boundary, access and matching expectations when defining the work.

Understanding the affected part of a fence

A timber fence is made up of palings or boards, rails, posts, caps, gates and connections to the ground or other structures. Visible damage may be limited to one panel, or it may indicate movement, decay, impact damage or a wider issue at posts and rails. We begin with the observed area and its connections, rather than assuming that a leaning fence can be resolved by replacing the most visible paling alone.

Repair versus replacement

MEL ONE inspects the fence boards, rails, posts and connections on site. We identify whether a local repair or a larger replacement suits the condition, access and matching requirements, then confirm the timber work and quote with you.

Posts, rails and ground conditions

Posts and rails carry the fence, so their condition influences the scope even when the visible problem is at the face. Soil, drainage, garden beds, retaining edges and previous repairs can affect access and the way a replacement is approached. Tell us if the fence is near a deck, paving, pool area, service line or mature planting. These details help identify practical constraints and avoid treating a boundary fence as an isolated decorative panel.

Matching an existing timber fence

When only part of a fence is being repaired, height, board style, rail arrangement and finish can influence how well new work sits beside old. Weathered timber may not match a new section immediately, and staining or painting is often a separate finishing decision. Photos taken from both sides of the fence help establish the visible layout. We discuss reasonable matching expectations and any profile or material decisions before work begins.

Boundary access and project responsibilities

Fence work can involve neighbours, side access, pets, landscaping and shared boundaries. MEL ONE discusses the property agreements, approvals and boundary information with the authorised owner or site contact before scheduling work. We confirm safe access, the timber repair scope and the sequence in your quote.

Fences beside decks and outdoor living areas

A fence can affect privacy, circulation, shade, gate placement and the way a deck or pergola meets the boundary. If the work connects to an outdoor-living upgrade, share the broader plan so posts, screens and edges are considered together. Coordinating related timber work early can reduce awkward junctions and repeated disturbance to finished landscaping. The scope should still distinguish fence work from decking, drainage, electrical and other trade packages.

Details for a useful fencing enquiry

Tell MEL ONE which fence sections need attention, what has moved or broken and any nearby gates, structures, slopes or garden beds. Safely taken photos and approximate lengths or heights are optional. We check posts, rails, panels, access and ground conditions on site, explain repair or replacement options and confirm the agreed scope and written quote.

Scope the affected fence section — questions

Will replacing palings straighten a leaning fence?

Not if the cause is failed posts, rails or ground movement. Assess the supporting parts and access before treating the visible boards as the whole repair.

Who agrees work on a shared boundary fence?

Confirm ownership, neighbour discussions, permission and cost arrangements before removal or replacement is booked. The carpentry quote does not settle a boundary dispute or establish a legal boundary.

Can new boards match an old fence exactly?

Profile and layout may be matched where materials are available, but new timber usually looks different from weathered boards. Discuss any wider coating work and the limits of colour matching in advance.
