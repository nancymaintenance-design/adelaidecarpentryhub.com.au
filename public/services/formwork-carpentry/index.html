Formwork Set-out for Slabs, Walls and Beams

Formwork shapes the concrete element, so the first decisions concern dimensions, levels, rebates, penetrations and the pour sequence. MEL ONE reviews the relevant issued drawings and site set-out before fabrication or erection. Foundation walls, slab edges, beams and feature shapes each need their own details rather than a single general formwork allowance.

Check where one pour or element meets the next and who supplies the confirmed set-out. Curved or raked work needs sufficient dimensional information to define the shape and intended finish. A missing junction or unclear revision should be resolved with the responsible designer or builder while changes remain practical.

Formwork Temporary Works and Bracing Responsibilities

Temporary formwork still needs a coordinated support and bracing system. The proposed element, access, pour arrangement and project documentation determine the responsibilities for temporary works and the erection sequence. Design questions and unresolved loads need the responsible professional input before the affected work proceeds.

Name who provides the temporary-works information and who checks readiness at each project hold point. Access platforms, lifting and material handling should be considered with the system, not added after fabrication. A formwork quote should describe the agreed supply, erection and support responsibilities rather than imply the carpenter controls every part of the pour.

Cast-in Items, Services and Pour Readiness

Sleeves, bolts, starter bars, openings and service penetrations need their locations checked before concrete placement. The quote and project programme should identify who installs each item and who confirms it is ready. Compare the relevant structural and service details early so a missing or conflicting item can be raised before the form is closed.

Pour readiness also depends on the other responsible trades and site team completing their checks. Concrete supply, reinforcement, placement and curing remain distinct interfaces unless expressly included in an agreed project scope. Record unresolved items and the person authorising the next stage instead of treating erected formwork as permission to pour.

Form-face Finish and Controlled Stripping

The selected form face, joints and ties influence the appearance of exposed concrete. Discuss the required visible finish with the builder, designer and concrete contractor before choosing the formwork system. An appearance expectation should be recorded in the specification; it is not established solely by calling the element architectural.

Stripping follows the project's engineering, temporary-works documentation and responsible site direction. Visual appearance or an assumed number of days is not a release criterion. Agree access, removal order, material handling and what happens at adjoining completed surfaces before the stripping stage is priced and scheduled.

Formwork Pricing Brief Inclusions

Identify the elements, dimensions, levels, current drawings, finish requirements and intended staged pours. Specify whether supply, fabrication, erection, stripping or a combination is required, and explain lifting and delivery constraints. These details allow a quote to describe the actual carpentry package instead of relying on a generic area rate.

A change to a rebate, penetration or pour stage can alter fabrication and erection even when the overall area is unchanged. Price those changes against the issued details before work proceeds. Keep pour-readiness checks, release directions and outstanding removal work with the site record.

Formwork Quotes, Pour Interfaces and Stripping Questions

Does the formwork quote include the concrete pour?

A formwork quote should say whether it covers supply, fabrication, erection, stripping or a combination. Concrete supply, reinforcement, placement and curing are separate interfaces unless expressly included in an agreed project scope. Name who installs cast-in items, checks the relevant details and authorises pour readiness; erected forms alone do not mean the wider project checks are complete.

Who determines when formwork can be stripped?

Release follows the project's engineering, temporary-works documentation and responsible site direction. Visual appearance or an assumed number of days is not a release criterion.

What formwork information is needed for pricing?

Provide the element dimensions, levels, issued drawings, finish requirements and whether supply, erection or stripping is required. Access, lifting and staged pours can change the labour and system needed.
