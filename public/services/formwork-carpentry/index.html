Formwork Carpentry

MEL ONE discusses timber formwork with the builder and project team before fabrication. Set-out, temporary supports, embedded items and pour readiness need a documented sequence and clearly assigned responsibilities.

Foundation wall formwork

Foundation, wall and beam formwork scope depends on structural details, temporary works and cast-in items. Agree the required system and responsible project checks before fabrication.

Slab & beam formwork

Slab and beam support systems follow the project design and temporary-works documentation. Specify the intended form face, access and erection and stripping responsibilities before pricing.

Feature-shape formwork

Curved, raked and irregular concrete elements built in timber to match architect detail.

Set-out before formwork is fabricated

Formwork begins with the current structural drawings, pour sequence and site set-out. We confirm dimensions, levels, penetrations, rebates, construction joints and access before cutting material. Early review is particularly important where multiple concrete elements meet, because a small error in one pour can affect the next trade and the visible finish.

Temporary works and bracing coordination

Formwork is temporary, but it must be treated as a serious site system. The support, bracing, pour rate, access and stripping sequence are considered with the project team and relevant documentation. We work within the agreed temporary-works approach and escalate items requiring engineering or site management direction rather than improvising structural decisions.

Embedded items and trade interfaces

Sleeves, bolts, starter bars, services and cast-in items need to be located before concrete is placed. We coordinate these interfaces against the issued information and identify clashes while adjustment is still possible. This reduces the risk of drilling, cutting or patching newly completed concrete because an item was missed or shifted at the last moment.

Form-face quality and concrete finish

The selected form face, joints, ties and release process influence the appearance of exposed concrete. Where finish expectations are important, we discuss them early with the builder, concrete contractor and designer so the formwork system is matched to the specified result. Concrete placement and curing remain specialist activities coordinated through the wider construction team.

Safe access and orderly stripping

Formwork operations need clear access, material handling space and a controlled removal sequence. We keep work areas organised and follow the agreed direction for inspection, pour readiness and stripping. No form is removed simply because it looks complete; timing and release must follow the project's engineering, program and site-safety requirements.

Scoping commercial formwork accurately

Useful information includes structural drawings, details, concrete programme, finish requirements, elevation or slab levels, access and lifting constraints, and whether the scope is supply, erect, strip or a combination. Sharing this early helps identify the right system and labour sequence for the project rather than relying on a generic rate that does not reflect real site conditions.

Discuss the pour interface — questions

Does the formwork quote include the concrete pour?

Concrete supply, reinforcement, placement and curing are separate interfaces unless expressly included in an agreed project scope. Identify who checks cast-in items and authorises pour readiness.

Who determines when formwork can be stripped?

Release follows the project's engineering, temporary-works documentation and responsible site direction. Visual appearance or an assumed number of days is not a release criterion.

What formwork information is needed for pricing?

Provide the element dimensions, levels, issued drawings, finish requirements and whether supply, erection or stripping is required. Access, lifting and staged pours can change the labour and system needed.
