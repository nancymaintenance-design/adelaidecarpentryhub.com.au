Heritage Carpentry

MEL ONE discusses heritage carpentry with retention as the starting point. Record original profiles and condition, then agree the repair approach and any heritage advice or approvals needed for the property.

Mortise & tenon repair

Dovetail, through, and groove joints inspected, repaired and remade with period-matched timber and hand tools.

Carved trim restoration

Existing cornices, eave brackets and carved panels are recorded before deciding what can be retained, locally repaired or replicated. Match the agreed profile and material without assuming all original fabric needs replacement.

Period window restoration

Period sash windows are assessed for retained timber, joints and operation. Agree hardware, glazing and coating interfaces against the repair scope and heritage requirements.

Record original fabric before intervention

Heritage timber carries information in its profiles, joints, glass, hardware and layers of finish. We begin by documenting the visible condition and identifying elements that can be retained, repaired or carefully replicated. This gives owners and project teams a clearer basis for deciding how to preserve the character of a place while addressing practical performance issues.

Understand approvals and project responsibilities

Heritage work can involve owner, architect, heritage adviser, builder and approval authority responsibilities that vary by property and scope. We do not assume a single rule applies to every Adelaide building. Instead, we work from the approved project information and coordinate the carpentry detail with the people responsible for heritage advice, design and approvals where these are required.

Repair first, replace only where justified

Sound original material often has value beyond its appearance. We assess whether local repairs, splices, consolidation, new sections or full replication are appropriate to the observed condition and intended performance. The aim is a clear, honest repair strategy—not a cosmetic replacement that loses defining proportions or evidence of the building's original construction.

Match profiles, species and detailing

When new timber is needed, profiles, grain direction, joinery detail and interfaces are considered alongside the existing work. Exact historic matching may not always be possible or appropriate, so decisions are discussed in the context of the approved design and available materials. Samples or templates can help resolve visible details before work proceeds across a larger elevation or repeated feature.

Windows, doors and ongoing care

Traditional openings depend on many small elements working together: sashes, cords, weights, stops, weathering details, glazing and paint systems. We review operation and visible condition as part of the carpentry scope, while specialist glazing, coatings or performance requirements are coordinated with the relevant trades. Ongoing inspection helps identify coating or water-entry issues before more substantial damage develops.

Arrange a heritage carpentry assessment

Tell MEL ONE the address, timber elements and concerns you have noticed. Existing heritage advice, approvals, drawings, history and safe photos help if available, but are optional for first contact. We assess the timber on site, identify what can be retained or repaired and confirm the restoration plan and quote, including qualified heritage input and approval requirements.

Prepare a heritage timber enquiry — questions

Must heritage timber be replaced to improve operation?

No. Sound original material may be retained while local damage, hardware or joints are repaired. The condition and conservation requirements should guide how much intervention is justified.

Can modern profiles replace original heritage trim?

Matching an original profile may matter to the building's character and any consent conditions. Record a sound reference section and agree materials, dimensions and finish before choosing a replacement.

Who confirms heritage approval requirements?

Requirements depend on the property, its status and the proposed work. Confirm them with the relevant authority and heritage adviser where needed; the carpentry quote should state who obtains approvals before work starts.
