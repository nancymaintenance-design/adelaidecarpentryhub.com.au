Architectural Joinery

Architectural joinery brings designed timber details into a room. MEL ONE discusses profiles, finish samples, supports and adjoining surfaces so bespoke features can be scoped before manufacture and installation.

Wall panelling

Material choice (Blackwood, American Oak) through to fixing — grooved modules, matched joins and open or closed grain finish.

Hidden & pivot doors

Flush hidden doors, recessed handles and frameless detail, with door-in-wall and non-load-bearing installation options.

Complex stair rails

Stair-rail and balustrade details need coordinated design, supports and installation responsibilities. Agree the specified system and any engineering or certification before fabrication; decorative joinery alone does not establish barrier performance.

VJ panelling, wainscoting and timber feature walls

Start with the intended appearance and the room, then compare board or sheet profiles, groove spacing, panel height and the selected finish. VJ-style panelling and wainscoting need junctions resolved at corners, skirtings, architraves and door openings. The material and substrate must suit the setting; a visual profile does not establish moisture, fire or acoustic performance. Confirm service locations, removable access and any electrical changes before fixing details are approved.

Agree the panel layout before ordering

A small layout drawing can show how grooves meet openings, where joints fall and which edges remain visible. Review a sample for grain, paint or coating expectations and note preparation and finishing responsibilities. This is a feature-joinery decision, not a kitchen-cabinet or wardrobe brief, so keep the relevant measurements, materials and installation interfaces with this scope.

Feature ceilings & light coves

Timber ceiling features, light coves and panels are coordinated with specified supports and services. Acoustic or fire performance requires relevant system evidence and specialist design.

Turning a design concept into buildable detail

Architectural joinery often starts with a drawing, sketch or reference image that still needs practical decisions. We review proportions, substrate, fixing zones, junctions, access and the sequence of adjoining finishes. This translation from visual intent to buildable detail is where careful joinery planning prevents a premium feature from becoming difficult to install or maintain.

Grain, colour and sample approval

Natural timber varies in grain, tone and figure; that variation is part of its character. We discuss the desired level of matching, board direction, veneer layout, stain or clear finish, and the effect of lighting before final selection. Samples and agreed reference points help clients understand what is being specified without promising that every piece of timber will be identical.

Substrates, movement and fixing

Large panels, doors, rails and linings must be detailed for their substrate and environment. We consider access to fixing points, realistic tolerances, movement between materials and the future need to reach concealed services. Where a design requires engineered support, glazing or specialist hardware, those elements are coordinated through the relevant project professionals.

Workshop preparation and site installation

Complex pieces benefit from a measured workflow: site verification, drawings or templates, workshop preparation, test fitting where practical, delivery protection and staged installation. This reduces surprise cutting on site and gives the team a controlled way to manage fine joins, shadow lines and continuous visual features across a room or commercial space.

Detail review in the finished light

Joinery is experienced at close range and under the project's actual lighting. At completion we review alignment, gaps, edge treatment, door operation and the intended visual rhythm of the feature. The goal is not artificial perfection from every angle; it is a well-resolved piece that reads as deliberate, balanced and appropriate to its material.

What to bring to an architectural joinery brief

Tell MEL ONE the feature and finish you want and the current project stage. We measure the site and confirm interfaces with hardware, lighting, stone or glazing, the manufacture and installation sequence and the written quote. Existing drawings, elevations, materials schedules, inspiration images and photos are optional for first contact. Final drawings, specifications and required approvals are agreed before manufacture.

Define the architectural detail — questions

How is architectural joinery different from kitchen cabinetry?

This service focuses on designed timber features and detailed interior interfaces. Use the kitchen-cabinetry service for cabinet installation and the storage service for wardrobes, bookcases and storage layouts.

Can a concealed door be added to any wall?

Wall construction, opening size, hardware, door weight and adjacent finishes need checking. Structural alterations and rated-door requirements need separate design and specialist responsibilities.

What should be agreed for VJ panelling or wainscoting?

Agree the panel profile, height, groove layout, substrate, corners and edges against the actual room. Samples help confirm the intended finish. Identify switches, sockets and access panels early; electrical changes and any claimed fire or acoustic performance require their own responsible specialists and evidence.
