Door Jambs & Interior Trim

MEL ONE scopes door-frame, jamb and architrave work for Adelaide repairs and renovations. Check whether the existing door can be reused and how the opening meets the wall, floor and trim before choosing local repair or replacement.

What interior trim work includes

Interior trim may include a new or repaired door jamb, architraves, reveals, stop details, casing, local lining repairs and junctions to skirtings. It is often part of second-fix carpentry, but it can also be a focused repair after a door change, wall alteration or water-damage rectification. We define the actual opening and adjoining finishes before deciding whether the work needs a local adjustment, replacement component or a coordinated finishing package.

Jambs, reveals and door operation

A door frame needs to work with the wall thickness, finished floor, leaf, hardware and swing. Small differences in plumb, floor level, lining thickness or hinge position can affect the final clearances. We review these practical relationships before final fixing and trim installation. Where a door is sticking, binding or no longer closing cleanly, photographs and notes about the change over time help distinguish an adjustment question from a wider jamb, leaf or surrounding-building issue.

Architraves and visible proportions

Architraves create the transition from opening to wall, so profile, width, corner treatment and the relationship to skirting are immediately visible. In a renovation, an existing profile may need to be matched or a new trim language chosen for the room. We use the observed room details, reference photos and finishes schedule to guide that decision. The aim is not to overcomplicate a simple opening, but to prevent new trim from looking disconnected from adjacent doors and rooms.

Existing walls and changed openings

Renovated homes may have patched linings, uneven wall thickness, former openings, new bulkheads or changed floor levels around a door. These conditions can influence the jamb depth, reveal detail and the amount of trim work needed to create a clean transition. We assess what is visible and identify where opening-up or another trade’s work may be needed before a final finish is possible. Clear scope boundaries avoid a trim allowance masking unresolved wall or structural changes.

Hardware and specialist requirements

Hinges, latches, locks, seals and handles need to be selected and coordinated with the door and frame. Tell us whether hardware is supplied, whether the opening has acoustic, security, fire or accessibility requirements in approved project documents, and whether another trade is managing those elements. We do not assume specialist performance requirements from appearance alone; identifying them early helps the relevant project team plan the right product, installation sequence and checks.

Second-fix sequencing

Door jamb and trim work normally sits between plastering, flooring, painting, cabinetry and door-hanging tasks. Starting before wall linings or floors are ready can create repeated work or inconsistent junctions. We discuss room readiness, material delivery, access and the finish responsibilities before installation. This is especially useful where multiple rooms are being renovated in stages and the owner needs a sensible order for doors, skirtings, built-ins and paint preparation.

Preparing an Adelaide trim enquiry

Tell MEL ONE the proposed door type, hardware, current build stage and trim that needs matching. Discuss the frame and trim — questions

Can I keep the door when replacing its jamb?

Possibly. Check the leaf condition, dimensions, hardware and required clearances against the proposed frame. Reuse should be agreed before sizing the replacement jamb.

Does a new door frame allow a wider opening?

Widening an opening can involve structural work, services and approvals. A trim or jamb replacement does not include authorisation to alter a load-bearing wall; resolve design and responsible trades first.

How do architraves meet existing skirting?

Compare the profiles, widths and finished floor level before selecting trim. Existing wall irregularity and paint build-up can affect returns and matching, so define any adjacent making-good in the quote.
