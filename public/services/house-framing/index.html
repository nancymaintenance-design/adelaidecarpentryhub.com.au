SERVICE 01

House Framing

Frame installation begins with the project design: wall frames, roof framing and floor members need coordinated drawings, specified connections and a clear handover to the next trade.

Wall frame construction

Wall framing follows the issued design, including member sizes, treatment suited to exposure and specified connections. Resolve design changes with the responsible designer before installation.

Roof trusses & rafters

Roof trusses and rafters follow project and supplier documentation. Connection, bracing, lifting and inspection responsibilities need confirmation with the builder and relevant designer.

Floor joist laying

Floor members, support details and connections follow the issued design. Moisture management and treatment depend on the floor system and exposure rather than a single rule for every build.

Structural alignment

Laser-levelled checks throughout for plumb, level and dimensional accuracy within building tolerances.

Reading the plans before timber arrives

A reliable frame starts with a complete set of current drawings. We review wall heights, openings, roof geometry, connection details and any engineer or truss documentation before work begins, then raise clashes early rather than leaving decisions to the day of installation. This gives builders and owners a clearer sequence for deliveries, trades and inspections.

Set-out for openings and services

Doors, windows, stair voids, wet-area penetrations and service runs all affect the frame. We set out these points from the approved drawings, check rough opening dimensions and coordinate where plumbing, electrical or mechanical work needs allowance. Care at this stage helps prevent later cutting, packing and patching that can compromise the finish.

Timber selection and site storage

The right member, treatment level and connection detail depend on its job and exposure. Timber is kept supported, protected from standing water and handled to limit unnecessary damage or twist before it is fixed. Where a design calls for proprietary components, we work to the supplier information and the project documentation rather than substituting by assumption.

Installation sequence at lock-up

Framing is planned around access, lifting, bracing, truss installation and the next trade. We keep the work area orderly and communicate practical hold points so that survey, engineering or building inspections can occur without avoidable delay. The aim is a frame that is ready for roof, cladding and internal trades to follow efficiently.

Checks before the next trade starts

Before handing an area forward, we visually review line, plumb, level, bracing, openings and obvious damage. MEL ONE identifies any work needed while it is accessible and discusses required inspections, engineering sign-off and the responsible project parties as part of the agreed framing scope.

What to include in a framing enquiry

Describe the build stage, access, preferred timing and supply responsibilities. Existing drawings are optional for initial contact; current design documents are needed before the affected work can be confirmed. Mention slopes, ties into existing structures and unusual roof geometry.

Prepare the framing stage — questions

Can framing start from architectural drawings alone?

Structural details, truss information and any unresolved connections need review with the responsible designer or engineer before the affected work begins. Architectural drawings alone may leave critical items unresolved.

Who checks the frame before it is covered?

Agree the required inspections and the responsible builder, engineer or certifier in advance. MEL ONE's carpentry checks do not replace statutory inspections or engineering sign-off.

What changes a framing quote?

Supply versus labour-only scope, member schedules, roof geometry, lifting access, bracing requirements and connections to existing work all affect the allowance. Identify the current drawing revision in the quote.
