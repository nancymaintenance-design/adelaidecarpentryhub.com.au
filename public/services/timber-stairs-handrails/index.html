Timber Stair, Handrail & Balustrade Repairs

MEL ONE repairs existing timber stairs, steps, handrails and balustrades in Adelaide. We distinguish a split tread or loose fixing from damage to stringers, platform supports or the whole stair, then agree local repairs and any assessed structural work.

Split stair treads and loose timber steps

Local stair work can cover treads, risers, platform boards and associated timber damaged by cracking, rot, movement or surface wear. A tread that moves underfoot needs its fixing and support checked, not just its visible face. Record which step is affected and whether the fault has changed. Visibly unstable steps should be kept out of use while an assessment and repair are arranged.

Treads, risers and stringers have different roles

The tread is the surface stepped on; the riser is the upright face between steps where present. Stringers are the inclined members supporting the stair, rather than decorative trims. This distinction affects the repair scope. Replacing a local surface component does not establish that a damaged stringer or platform support is sound, and it does not turn a full stair rebuild into a small maintenance job.

Handrails are not the same as balustrades

A handrail provides a graspable support; a balustrade is a protective barrier at an edge or alongside a stair. Posts, balusters, top and bottom rails and their fixing points can all form part of an existing timber barrier repair. A loose handrail fixing and a moving barrier post may require different work. Safety-related parts are assessed for their intended role, not described as purely decorative.

Repairing timber and connections together

Work can include replacement of confirmed damaged components and failed connections, with inspection of the timber receiving the new fixing. Simply fitting new screws into deteriorated timber may leave the underlying problem unresolved. We record the retained material, component match and connection scope. A heavier or altered replacement component needs its effect on the existing arrangement considered before it is ordered.

Movement that needs structural assessment

Damage to stringers, platform supports or an entire stair that has shifted is separated from local tread repair. The repair approach and relevant professional responsibilities must be established before structural work proceeds. Widespread decay can also make the visible fault an incomplete picture. MEL ONE records the assessed scope, inaccessible areas and any further investigation needed before confirming the repair.

Common enquiries from occupied homes

A back step with a cracked edge, an old stair that creaks with one moving tread, and a handrail that shifts when a family member uses it are different enquiries. Describe the location and observed movement without testing an unsafe part. Keep people away from seriously loose barriers or unstable steps. MEL ONE can arrange an inspection and explain the local repair options and any wider assessment needed.

Surface finish, slip treatment and appearance

Quote the non-slip treatment and coating scope separately from component repair. Replacement timber, retained weathered surfaces and old finishes can differ in appearance. Confirm preparation, compatible protection and any local or broader recoating before work begins. A surface treatment is not a substitute for repairing damaged supports or loose safety-critical fixings.

Local repair versus a complete stair rebuild

A replacement tread, several barrier components and a rebuilt flight are different scopes and should be priced separately. List component quantities, retained supports, access, protection, removal, finishing and professional interfaces. If opening up reveals more damage, agree the extra work and cost before proceeding. Existing photos or drawings are optional for the initial enquiry; site assessment determines the agreed repair brief.

Timber Stair, Handrail & Balustrade Repairs: questions answered

What is the difference between a stair tread and a stringer?

A tread is the walking surface; a stringer supports the steps along the stair. Damage to a stringer is not covered simply by replacing a tread and should be assessed as a structural concern.

Why is a loose timber balustrade more than a cosmetic repair?

A balustrade acts as a protective barrier. Loose posts, decayed timber or failed connections can affect that role, so severely loose areas should have use restricted and be assessed before the repair scope is agreed.

Can a damaged tread be replaced without rebuilding the staircase?

Local replacement may be suitable when the retained supports and surrounding components are assessed as appropriate. A full flight rebuild, damaged stringer or moving platform is a separate scope, not automatically included in a tread repair.
