Outdoor Living

New outdoor carpentry should suit how you use the yard and how it meets the house. MEL ONE discusses decking and pergola scope, material choices, access and the design responsibilities before work is scheduled.

Timber decking

Compare decking species and profiles alongside drainage, ventilation, exposure and the selected finish. Pool edges and other special locations need their own design and performance review.

Pergolas & shade structures

Freestanding or attached pergolas need a design suited to the site. Roof coverings, shade equipment and planting loads should be identified before connections and materials are selected.

Fencing

Fence and privacy-screen details can be coordinated with a new outdoor area. Materials, supports and finishing depend on exposure, boundary arrangements and site conditions.

New decks and new pergolas are different briefs

For a new deck, describe the usable area, levels, access, adjoining doors and preferred board appearance. For a new pergola, describe whether the aim is shade, shelter or a framed garden space, along with any proposed covering or equipment. The design, supports, drainage and project responsibilities follow the actual proposal. Existing deck restoration and damaged pergola timber should be assessed as repair enquiries rather than treated as a new-build package.

Timber or composite decking: compare the complete system

Compare the selected product’s intended use, appearance, heat and slip characteristics, fixing system and maintenance instructions. Timber species and composite products vary, so neither material name establishes a universal lifespan, maintenance saving or site suitability. Frame design, ventilation, drainage, board spacing and supports must follow the approved design and product requirements. Ask what replacement boards, coatings or future repair involve before choosing the finish.

Timber privacy screens and batten layouts

A privacy-screen brief should identify the sightline to screen, desired openness, location and surrounding access. Batten spacing, supports and finishing affect appearance and upkeep; an attractive screen is not automatically a wind barrier or safety barrier. Confirm the proposed screen scope, boundary arrangements, fixing conditions and any required design input before committing to a layout.

Materials for the actual exposure

Treatment, fasteners and coatings are selected for actual exposure and the specified system. Coastal conditions warrant discussion of corrosion, drainage and ongoing care rather than a universal material prescription.

Start with how the space will be used

A deck for quiet meals, a pool edge, a shaded play zone and an entertaining area all need different layouts, circulation and finishes. We ask how many people use the space, where furniture and barbecue equipment will sit, how the house opens to the yard and what needs to remain accessible before proposing a board direction or structure.

Levels, drainage and ground clearance

Outdoor timber lasts better when water can move away and air can circulate below it. We assess existing levels, thresholds, drainage paths, garden edges and access to subfloor areas so the finished deck does not create a practical water trap. Site conditions determine the appropriate substructure, footing approach and transitions to paving or lawn.

Board, fixing and finish choices

Species, profile, colour, fasteners and coating should be chosen together, not as isolated showroom decisions. We explain the visual and maintenance implications of common timber options, exposed versus concealed fixing, and oil or coating systems. This helps clients balance initial appearance, barefoot comfort, future touch-ups and the character that real timber develops outdoors.

Pergola and privacy integration

A pergola, screen or fence works best when it is designed with the deck rather than added after it. We consider shade direction, posts, fixings, privacy lines, lighting and the relationship to existing walls and services. Where approvals, engineering or boundary requirements apply, these need to be resolved through the appropriate project process before construction proceeds.

A practical maintenance plan

Maintenance is easier when it is planned at handover. We can discuss cleaning, clearing leaves from gaps, watching for coating wear, keeping drainage paths open and checking high-exposure areas. The right interval depends on species, finish, sunlight, weather and use, so we avoid one-size-fits-all promises and focus on what the finished space actually needs.

Preparing an outdoor-living brief

Tell MEL ONE the outdoor area, features and style you want. We measure the site and check access, levels and connections to the house before confirming design, preparation and the written quote. Plan a new deck or pergola — questions

Should I choose timber or composite boards for a new deck?

Compare actual products against the site, desired appearance, cleaning and maintenance requirements, fixing system and future repairs. Neither timber nor composite is suitable simply because of its label. The selected board, frame and drainage details need to work together under the project design and manufacturer requirements.

Can a pergola be attached to any existing wall?

The existing construction, load path and proposed design must be checked. Attachment, engineering and approval requirements need resolution before construction; a convenient wall is not sufficient evidence.

Which outdoor timber needs the least upkeep?

Exposure, board profile, finish and drainage influence care as well as species. Compare cleaning and recoating needs for the actual location rather than assuming a timber is maintenance-free.
