Accessible Timber Entry: Begin with the Person and Route

Accessibility carpentry begins with the movement the person needs to make, whether on foot, with a walking aid or using a wheelchair. Describe the difficult step or threshold and how the entrance is used each day. A timber ramp is one possible part of the solution, so the approach, doorway and space beyond need consideration together.

Existing access assessments or specified recommendations can help shape the brief but are optional for first contact. If a formal accessibility outcome is required, identify that need early so the relevant assessment and design responsibilities can be resolved. No single entrance alteration establishes that every requirement or individual need has been met.

Doorway, Landing and Timber Threshold Adjustments

Door swing, available width, entrance height, landing space and the ground approach all affect a useful transition. Lowering or repairing a timber threshold may still leave an awkward turn or door movement. MEL ONE assesses the proposed adjustment and how it meets adjoining floors and the outside landing before confirming the carpentry scope.

A damaged threshold can require repair or replacement before a transition is planned. The door operation, weather junction and retained materials need checking together. Structural alteration, weatherproofing and other specialist interfaces should be identified separately where the proposal extends beyond ordinary timber adjustment.

Timber Ramp and Handrail Planning: A Complete Fixed Route

A fixed timber ramp or platform needs a layout showing the approach, turning space, door movement and connection to the ground. These details establish the usable route and the supporting work. Compare the proposed layout with the person's needs before the timber and components are ordered.

The agreed project design specifies supports, foundation work, entrance junctions, non-slip measures, handrails and edge protection. Resolve applicable approvals and professional responsibilities before construction. A convenience improvement and a project requiring a formal accessibility outcome can need different assessment and documentation.

Repairing an Existing Ramp and Keeping Access Available

Existing ramps can have damaged boards, supporting timber, connections or entrance junctions. Compare local repair and more extensive replacement against the accessible condition and required design. Changed movement needs matter too: restoring the old arrangement may not address how the entrance is now used.

Load-bearing repairs need the relevant assessment, design and temporary-support arrangements before affected work proceeds. Discuss which entrance can be used during the work and any separately arranged trade tasks. A planned installation or repair sequence is especially important where the doorway is the person's main usable route.

An Accessibility Carpentry Quote and Handover

The quote should describe the assessed route, specified ramp or threshold work, supports, foundations, handrails, edge protection and surface treatment. Include access, protection, removal, waste and adjoining making-good, with outstanding design or specialist responsibilities named. A partial board repair and a fully specified new route cover different outcomes.

Route length, entrance junctions, supports and access during construction affect the price. Compare these with specified handrails, edge protection and surface treatment. At handover, review the timber work against the agreed design, keep care information and record remaining professional checks or adjoining work.

Thresholds, Ramp Inclusions and Accessibility Questions

Is lowering a timber threshold enough to improve an entrance?

It may be one part of the work, but the person's movement needs, entrance height, door swing, doorway width, landing and ground conditions must be considered together. MEL ONE assesses the proposed timber adjustment and adjoining junctions before confirming the scope. A formal accessibility outcome needs the relevant assessment and specified design rather than an assumption based on threshold height alone.

What should a fixed timber ramp quote include?

The quote should identify the agreed ramp and platform layout, supporting structure, foundation work and entrance junctions. It should also state the specified non-slip measures, handrails and edge protection, with any separate work or unresolved design responsibility made clear. MEL ONE confirms those inclusions around the assessed site and project design before work begins.

Does installing a ramp automatically make the entrance compliant?

No. A ramp or threshold change may improve convenience without establishing compliance with every applicable requirement. A project needing a formal accessibility outcome must resolve the person-and-site assessment, specified design, applicable approvals and relevant professional responsibilities. MEL ONE scopes the timber work against those confirmed requirements rather than offering a universal compliance guarantee.
