Accessibility Carpentry & Timber Ramps

MEL ONE provides accessibility carpentry in Adelaide, including threshold repairs and adjustments, timber ramps and platforms. We start with how the person approaches and uses the entrance, then assess the site and confirm the design, inclusions and relevant professional or approval requirements for the proposed work.

Start with the person using the entrance

Accessibility carpentry begins with the intended user and the task they need to complete. Tell MEL ONE whether the entrance is used on foot, with a walker or with a wheelchair, and describe the point that causes difficulty. The approach, doorway and space beyond it need to be considered together. A useful brief explains the movement required instead of assuming that adding a ramp will resolve every part of entering and leaving the home.

Assess the doorway and approach together

Entrance height is only one part of planning a timber transition or ramp. MEL ONE considers the existing door swing, doorway width, landing space and ground conditions alongside the proposed route. An improvement at the threshold can still leave a difficult turn or an awkward door movement if those relationships are overlooked. The onsite assessment establishes what the proposed carpentry must meet and which questions need a specified design or relevant professional input.

Repair or adjust a timber threshold

A damaged timber threshold may need repair or replacement before a useful entrance transition can be planned. MEL ONE can also carry out agreed threshold adjustments where the assessed doorway and specified approach support them. The scope considers how the change meets the door, adjoining floor and outside landing. Door operation and the surrounding junction need review together so that a proposed transition is not treated as an isolated piece of timber.

Plan a new timber ramp or platform

MEL ONE builds timber ramps and platforms to the agreed project design and scope. The proposal needs to describe the route, the intended use and how the new work meets the entrance and ground. Space for approaching, turning and operating the door is considered during planning rather than left until the frame is built. Dimensions and details are confirmed for the person and site through the relevant assessment and design process.

Assess an existing ramp before repair

An existing timber ramp or platform may need repair to its boards, supporting timber, connections or entrance junction. MEL ONE assesses the accessible condition and discusses whether local repair or a more extensive replacement is appropriate. Tell us about changed movement needs as well as damaged timber, because restoring the old arrangement may not address how the entrance is now used. Any proposed change to layout or supporting work is defined before it becomes part of the repair scope.

List fixed-ramp inclusions clearly

A fixed timber ramp quote should identify the supporting structure, foundation work and junctions included in the project. Non-slip measures, handrails and edge protection also need explicit consideration and agreed inclusions. These details affect both the design and the completed route, so they should not be left to assumptions about the word ramp. The written scope explains the specified components, who resolves outstanding details and what adjoining work is separately arranged.

Confirm assessment, design and approvals

A convenience improvement does not by itself establish compliance with every applicable accessibility or building requirement. The project needs a person-and-site assessment, specified design and consideration of applicable approvals and relevant professional responsibilities. MEL ONE confirms the carpentry work against those agreed requirements before construction or alteration begins. If a formal accessibility outcome is needed, identify it in the brief so the appropriate assessment and documentation can be resolved before choosing the timber solution.

Plan installation and everyday access

An entrance project affects how the home is used while work is underway as well as after completion. Tell MEL ONE which doorway is normally used and whether another suitable route is available during the agreed work period. We discuss the installation sequence, access arrangements and the handover scope before scheduling the carpentry. Existing doors, thresholds, landings and any separately arranged trade work are coordinated so the completed timber work can be reviewed against the agreed design and inclusions.

Threshold, ramp and access-planning questions

Is lowering a timber threshold enough to improve an entrance?

It may be one part of the work, but the person's movement needs, entrance height, door swing, doorway width, landing and ground conditions must be considered together. MEL ONE assesses the proposed timber adjustment and adjoining junctions before confirming the scope. A formal accessibility outcome needs the relevant assessment and specified design rather than an assumption based on threshold height alone.

What should a fixed timber ramp quote include?

The quote should identify the agreed ramp and platform layout, supporting structure, foundation work and entrance junctions. It should also state the specified non-slip measures, handrails and edge protection, with any separate work or unresolved design responsibility made clear. MEL ONE confirms those inclusions around the assessed site and project design before work begins.

Does installing a ramp automatically make the entrance compliant?

No. A ramp or threshold change may improve convenience without establishing compliance with every applicable requirement. A project needing a formal accessibility outcome must resolve the person-and-site assessment, specified design, applicable approvals and relevant professional responsibilities. MEL ONE scopes the timber work against those confirmed requirements rather than offering a universal compliance guarantee.
