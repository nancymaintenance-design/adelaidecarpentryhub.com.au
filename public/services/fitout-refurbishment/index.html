SERVICE 05

Fitout & Refurbishment

MEL ONE scopes commercial carpentry for offices, retail and hospitality spaces around the premises and operating requirements. Timber partitions, feature finishes and displays need coordination with services, landlord conditions and the responsible project team.

Timber partition systems

Timber-faced partition details are scoped against project drawings. Structural, acoustic, fire and service requirements need identified design and specialist interfaces; demountability depends on the selected system.

Feature & suspended ceilings

Timber feature ceilings and light coves need coordination with supports, service access and the specified ceiling system. Electrical, mechanical and fire-service work remains with the responsible trades.

Shopfront & display walls

Retail timber feature walls, brand displays and custom furniture with commercial-grade hard-wearing surfaces.

Fitout planning around the operating business

An office, café, clinic or retail space has to keep functioning around deliveries, staff, customers and building rules. We begin by understanding the trading requirements, access hours, noisy-work restrictions, landlord process and opening date. That context informs a realistic carpentry sequence instead of assuming every commercial site can be treated like an empty shell.

Reading drawings, finishes and brand intent

Commercial drawings often combine architectural, services, fire, joinery and signage information. We review the details relevant to our work, confirm visible finishes and identify interfaces before fabrication or site installation. This helps protect a designer's intent while keeping practical questions—such as service access, durability and cleanability—visible to the project team.

Sequencing around services and approvals

Ceilings, partitions, feature linings and display elements must work around mechanical, electrical, hydraulic and fire services. We coordinate our installation sequence with the builder and other trades, and rely on the approved project information for matters that require specialist design, certification or authority approval. Clear hold points reduce avoidable rework in busy fitout programs.

Durable finishes for high-touch spaces

A customer-facing counter, wall lining or display must suit its daily use, not only its opening-day appearance. We discuss edge details, impact-prone corners, cleaning methods, access panels and repairability when selecting materials and finishes. These decisions give owners a more practical result in areas exposed to bags, chairs, trolleys, cleaning and frequent contact.

Occupied-site communication

Where work proceeds near staff or customers, orderly staging matters. We communicate with the responsible site contact about access, dust control expectations, protection, deliveries and day-to-day handovers. The exact controls depend on the building and scope, but the principle is constant: make work visible, coordinated and respectful of the people using the premises.

Briefing a commercial refurbishment

Tell MEL ONE the premises, project stage, opening deadline and desired carpentry work. We assess the site, occupied areas and interfaces with other trades, then confirm the carpentry package and written quote. Existing drawings, tenancy requirements, programme and photos help if available; we agree any required approvals and final specifications before work begins.

Brief the commercial timber scope — questions

Can a business remain open during carpentry work?

That depends on the work zone, public separation, noise, dust and safe access. Agree staging with the premises manager; continued trading is not assumed when defining the programme.

Are acoustic or fire performance ratings included?

Only a specified and appropriately designed system can support a performance requirement. Identify specialist design, installation and certification responsibilities rather than treating timber finishes as proof of a rating.

What should a commercial carpentry quote separate?

List timber work, finishes, removal, protection, delivery restrictions and handover areas. Keep electrical, mechanical, fire services, approvals and other trades clearly assigned.
