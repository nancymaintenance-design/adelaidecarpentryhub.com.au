Weatherboard Profile, Thickness and Exposed Width

Matching weatherboards involves the board's shape and thickness as well as its overall width. Exposed width is the portion visible after the boards overlap, so two similar-sized products can create different horizontal lines. Check the existing profile and overlap before specifying the replacement.

Plan the repair against adjoining boards, corners and trims so the new work forms a coherent local section. Board Overlaps, Trim Edges and Finishing

Replacement boards need their overlap and alignment coordinated with the run above, below and beside them. Window trims and corners often define the repair boundaries. Identify any trim removal, repair or reinstatement needed for access and the finished junction.

Local Board Replacement, Profile Matching and Leak Questions

Can one damaged weatherboard be replaced?

Yes, MEL ONE provides single-board and multiple-board local replacement. The workable repair extent depends on adjoining timber, fixing positions, board profile and nearby trims. An onsite assessment establishes whether an individual board repair is suitable or whether a local group needs replacement to form the agreed junctions.

Why does exposed board width matter when matching weatherboards?

Exposed width is the part of each board visible after it overlaps the next board. Matching only overall width can leave different horizontal lines or an unsuitable meeting with the existing run. MEL ONE considers profile, thickness, exposed width, overlap and alignment together when specifying replacement boards.

Will replacing boards resolve a hidden leak around a window?

That cannot be assumed from the damaged board alone. The assessment considers accessible backing, framing and waterproof interfaces, and opening the area may reveal further work.
