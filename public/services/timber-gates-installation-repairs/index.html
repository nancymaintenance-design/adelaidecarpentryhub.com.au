Gate Dragging on the Ground: Check the Leaf and Supports

A timber gate dragging across paving can have worn hinges, a moving post, a distorted leaf or changed ground clearance. MEL ONE checks the leaf, post, rails, bracing and hardware as an assembly. Where the latch also misses, its alignment should be considered with the movement instead of simply relocating the latch.

Describe when the fault began, whether it changes with weather and whether nearby paving or landscaping has changed. A sagging gate may be repairable when sound material and suitable supports remain. Significant decay or distortion can justify replacement, but new hinges alone do not establish that the rest of the opening is suitable.

Timber Gate Repairs and New Side Gates

A repair can involve agreed local timber work, suitable hardware replacement or adjustment after the cause is assessed. Check the retained frame and fixing base before adding parts to damaged material. The quote should distinguish the repaired leaf from any supporting-post or adjoining fence work.

For a new gate, plan clear width, opening direction and the route for bins, garden equipment and everyday access. Privacy, sight lines and the weight of the proposed leaf affect the design and supports. Measure the full opening and ground sweep rather than choosing a gate only by its nominal width.

Latch Alignment, Hinges and Specialist Equipment

Hinges and latches need to suit the gate, exposure, fixing material and intended use. Identify whether existing parts will be retained and who supplies replacements. Hardware that looks similar may still have different mounting or adjustment requirements.

Pool-barrier use, security systems, automation, intercoms and electrical access equipment need separate specialist assessment and coordination. Ordinary timber gate fitting does not establish pool-barrier or security performance. Where equipment is planned, resolve the timber leaf and support requirements with the responsible installer before manufacture or adjustment.

Gate Clearances, Fencing and Finish

Check the swing against paving, steps, planting, a deck and the adjoining fence. A new leaf may still catch if the supporting arrangement or ground relationship remains unresolved. For a shared access or boundary, agree permissions and working arrangements with the responsible contact before removal.

Discuss material, board pattern, weather exposure and coating with the gate design. Replacement timber and retained fencing can look different, so define matching expectations and any wider finishing work. At completion, review movement, ground clearance and latch engagement within the agreed repair or installation scope.

Sagging Gates, Pool Barriers and Automation Questions

Will new hinges fix a sagging timber gate?

New hinges can help where failed or unsuitable hardware is the cause, but post movement, leaf distortion, damaged rails and changed ground levels can also make a gate sag. Check the assembled opening and the material receiving the fixings first. The agreed repair may involve adjustment, local timber work, supports or replacement, with latch alignment reviewed after the cause is addressed.

Can a garden gate be used as a pool barrier gate?

Pool barriers have specific requirements for the complete system. Identify the location before quoting and arrange appropriate assessment; ordinary timber gate work does not establish pool-barrier compliance.

Does timber gate work include automation?

Automation, intercoms, power and access-control equipment need specialist scope and coordination. Agree the timber leaf and support requirements with the responsible installer before manufacture or adjustment.
