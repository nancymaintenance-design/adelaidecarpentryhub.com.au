Subfloor Timber Repairs

MEL ONE provides local subfloor timber repairs in Adelaide, beginning with a safe, accessible inspection. We distinguish floorboard and fixing problems from faults in the supporting timber, then define the approved repair and any design, temporary support or professional assessment needed before load-bearing work begins.

Describe a dip, bounce or squeak precisely

A dipping floor, springy area or repeated squeak is a useful observation rather than a diagnosis. Note the room, the position of the symptom and whether it has changed over time. A floorboard, its fixing and the timber supporting it are different repair tasks, even when the sound appears in the same place. MEL ONE uses the observed problem to guide the inspection instead of assuming every noisy floor requires structural strengthening.

Establish safe inspection access

The initial assessment depends on whether the relevant subfloor area can be reached safely. Tell us about existing access openings, restricted spaces and any known moisture or other conditions beneath the floor. MEL ONE inspects the safely accessible area and identifies limitations before describing the findings. If a part cannot be seen or reached, that uncertainty is recorded rather than presenting the inspection as a complete view of all concealed timber.

Distinguish the floor surface from its support

A repair decision needs to separate the floorboards and their fixings from the bearers, joists and connections below. Local movement at a board can require a different response from deterioration at a support end. We consider the accessible relationships between these parts and the location of the reported symptom. Where the cause cannot be confirmed from the initial inspection, further assessment is defined before a particular repair method is promised.

Inspect bearers, joists and connections

MEL ONE examines accessible bearers, joists, support ends, joints and connections relevant to the affected floor area. The assessment considers visible moisture, decay, deformation and movement as well as the condition of the timber itself. A connection problem and a deteriorated member may require different approved repairs. Findings are used to define the local work and to identify where assessment by a relevant professional is needed to resolve structural questions.

Define approved local timber repairs

The agreed work can include approved local strengthening, timber replacement or connection updates. The repair is specified around the actual condition and the confirmed design requirements rather than a generic promise to stiffen every floor. MEL ONE sets out the affected members or connections, access arrangements and responsibilities before work begins. If the exposed condition differs from the assessment, the repair scope is reviewed before extending work to additional supporting timber.

Resolve load-bearing work before starting

Work to load-bearing timber needs a confirmed design, a considered temporary-support sequence and relevant professional assessment. These responsibilities are established before the affected supporting member or connection is altered. The carpentry scope identifies the work MEL ONE will carry out and the project documents or professional input it relies on. A floor symptom or a photograph is not sufficient information to prescribe a structural alteration or promise a particular finished floor level.

Include floor access and reinstatement

Some local repairs may require access through the floor as part of the agreed work. Discuss the opening location, affected boards and reinstatement before assuming that all access can occur from underneath. The quote identifies the carpentry reinstatement and any adjoining finish work that needs a separate allowance. Tell MEL ONE about occupied rooms and planned floor finishing so the repair sequence can be coordinated with everyday use and the next trade.

Separate local repair from whole-house correction

Local subfloor timber repair does not include whole-house lifting, full floor levelling, restumping or foundation strengthening. Structural redesign and pest treatment are also separate project or specialist responsibilities. Where the inspection suggests a broader issue, the next assessment is discussed before a local repair is treated as a complete remedy. This keeps the quote tied to the timber work that has actually been assessed and avoids promising a building-wide result from a limited repair.

Subfloor inspection and repair questions

Does a squeaky or bouncing floor mean the joists need replacing?

No single symptom establishes that conclusion. Floorboards, fixings, supporting members and connections can produce different movement or noise problems. MEL ONE inspects the safely accessible area to distinguish those possibilities and defines any further professional assessment before confirming local strengthening, replacement or a connection repair.

Can MEL ONE repair load-bearing subfloor timber?

MEL ONE carries out approved local subfloor timber repairs, including work to supporting members where the required design and professional assessment are confirmed. Load-bearing work also needs a planned temporary-support sequence before alteration begins. The written scope identifies the approved repair, access, responsibilities and any floor reinstatement.

Will a local subfloor repair level the whole house?

A local repair is scoped to the assessed timber or connection problem and does not promise whole-house levelling. House lifting, full levelling, restumping, foundation strengthening and structural redesign are separate projects. If the inspection indicates a wider condition, the relevant assessment and next steps need to be resolved before choosing a repair.
