About MEL ONE — Adelaide Carpentry & Joinery

MEL ONE provides Adelaide carpentry, joinery, repair and renovation services. We assess or measure the work on site, confirm the materials, scope and written quote, and coordinate the agreed work through to handover.

From Timber Enquiry to Agreed Work Scope

MEL ONE supports homeowners, property managers, builders and businesses across Adelaide with on-site carpentry, workshop joinery, cabinetry, timber repair and maintenance. We begin with the condition of the property and the practical outcome the client needs: a safer deck, a workable kitchen, a repaired opening, a durable fitout or a well-resolved custom timber feature. Good work starts before tools arrive. We ask for the suburb, photos, drawings where available, access details and the decisions that matter most to the people who will use the space. That gives the project a clearer scope, helps coordinate the right trades and reduces avoidable surprises after work has started. Our approach is straightforward: explain what is known, identify what still needs checking, document the agreed work and keep communication practical as the job progresses.

C

Check the existing conditions

Our work spans structural carpentry, fine joinery, heritage repair and routine maintenance. We approach every site as a set of real conditions to be measured and understood: exposure, access, existing construction, services, finishes and the way the property is used. That practical attention is what turns a broad request into a workable carpentry scope.

  • Site carpentry, custom joinery and maintenance planning in one conversation
  • Measured work that accounts for existing walls, floors, services and access
  • Site conditions considered, including sun, moisture, exposure and older-home details
  • Specialist or regulated work identified before booking so responsibilities are clear
R

Record inclusions and open questions

Clear information is part of quality. We explain the intended work, inclusions, site assumptions and the points that need further investigation before a client commits. If a project includes specialist or regulated work, the written scope should identify the responsible contractor and the necessary information before work begins.

  • Scope and inclusions discussed before booking
  • Practical notice of access, material and sequencing requirements
  • Visible conditions and potential concealed conditions explained clearly
  • Completion review focused on operation, interfaces and agreed finishes
K

Keep a usable handover record

Timber work can form part of staged home improvements, agent maintenance or a builder’s programme. We record the agreed work and explain care and any outstanding tasks after completion, so clients have useful information for planning later repairs or improvements.

  • A direct point of contact for new enquiries
  • On-site assessment to check the property, timber condition and repair requirements
  • Homeowners, property managers, builders and businesses can discuss staged work
  • Care and maintenance considerations included where they affect the result

CUSTOMER PRIORITIES

Site Conditions, Access and Selections

Tell us which rooms must stay in use, what condition is visible and which selections are still open. Those details shape access, the trade sequence and quote assumptions.

Living or trading in the property

Access, dust, noise, pets, parking and rooms that must stay operational influence the practical staging of a home, tenancy or commercial project. Those details should be part of the initial brief, not an afterthought once materials arrive.

Existing conditions

Older alterations, uneven walls, concealed services, moisture paths and prior repairs can affect the work. Contact MEL ONE to arrange an on-site assessment, identify the cause and confirm the repair plan and quote. We agree any further access or staged work with you before proceeding.

Selections and lead times

Cabinetry, hardware, timber, glazing, flooring and made-to-order materials need to align with the project sequence. Selecting a finish is more useful when the dimensions, interfaces, installation access and timing are also understood.
